Part I – Operating Manual

This part explains how to set up and use an EBCTester with the original board: connecting the tester, logging in, entering batteries, running manual tests, test plans and internal-resistance measurements, and reviewing and managing the recorded data. No programming or electronics knowledge is needed – the device is ready to use as delivered.

This part explains how to bring a self-built EBCTester into operation and use it from day to day: wiring requirements, installing the firmware, configuring the hardware in Maker Settings, activating the configuration, and then running tests and managing the recorded data.

Introduction

What EBCTester does

ZKE (ZKETECH) EBC battery testers are popular, affordable instruments for charging and discharging batteries with constant current, constant power or constant voltage and for measuring capacity. Normally they are controlled by the manufacturer's Windows software, which means a PC has to stay connected for the whole test – often for days.

EBCTester replaces that PC. A small controller with an ESP32-S3 microcontroller is connected to the tester's serial interface. It controls the tester, runs complete test plans on its own and records every measurement in a database on a microSD card. You operate it with any web browser – from a phone, a tablet or a PC – either through your home Wi-Fi or through the device's own Wi-Fi access point. You can close the browser at any time: the test continues and keeps being recorded.

Main features

  • No PC needed during a test. Manual operations and automatic test plans run on the ESP32-S3.
  • Offline recording. All measurements go into an SQLite database on a FAT32 microSD card. Recording needs a ready SD card, not an Internet connection.
  • Browser interface. Charts, battery records, test control and administration. Everything, including the chart library, is served by the device itself; no cloud service and no Internet access are required.
  • Test plans with up to 10 steps: constant-current discharge, constant-power discharge, CC/CV charge, wait and repeat.
  • Internal resistance (DCIR) by a load-pulse test with three separate results (by default after 1, 5 and 10 seconds), optionally with automatic preparation to a target state of charge.
  • Test-run management: search, filter, rename and delete recorded runs.
  • Optional backup to a MariaDB server (for example on a NAS), Home Assistant integration via MQTT and a live debug log.
  • Manual firmware updates over Wi-Fi: new versions are announced, but only installed after you confirm.
  • Languages: English and German built in; additional languages can be loaded from the SD card.
  • Maker Settings: all hardware-related settings (UART and SD pins, LED, network and update defaults) are configured in the browser and stored on the device – no rebuild of the firmware is needed for your own wiring.

Factory and Maker devices

The same firmware runs on the original EBCTester board and on self-built devices. At the very first start it decides which of two modes applies, based on the level of GPIO 47:

ModeDetected whenBehaviour
FactoryModeGPIO 47 is pulled high (original board: solder jumper JP1 closed to 3.3 V, as delivered) Starts immediately with the pin assignment of the original board. All functions are available at once.
MakerModeGPIO 47 is low or not connected Starts with all hardware outputs switched off. Only the Wi-Fi access point and the web interface run until the maker has entered and activated the hardware configuration in Maker Settings.

This manual describes the Factory Edition. If you have built your own device, use the Maker Edition of this manual instead – it also covers the configuration of your hardware and contains a detailed technical reference.

This manual describes the Maker Edition. Owners of an original EBCTester board in its delivered state use the Factory Edition. The operation of the battery-tester functions is identical in both editions; the Maker Edition additionally covers the hardware configuration and contains the detailed technical reference.

The mode is detected only once and then stored. It is detected again only after the device configuration has been reset (see Device reset).

Supported testers

The firmware recognises a range of ZKE models by the identifier in their status messages. Only the models marked "enabled" can be controlled; all others are shown as read-only telemetry until their operation has been confirmed.

ModelMax. chargeMax. dischargeMax. powerStatus
EBC-A2018 V / 5 A30 V / 20 A85 Wenabled
EBC-A40L5 V / 40 A5 V / 40 A200 Wenabled
EBC-A50L5 V / 50 A5 V / 50 A250 Wenabled
EBC-A05(+), EBC-A10(+/H), EBC-A20+, EBD-M10, EBD-A10H, EBD-M03(+), EBD-M05, EBD-A100(L), EBC-A100Lrecognisedread-only

These limits are enforced for every manual command, every plan step and every internal-resistance parameter. The A40L and A50L are low-voltage testers (max. 5 V): use them only with single lithium cells and settings that suit the cell. EBCTester has no battery-chemistry selection – the values you enter must be right for your battery.

NoteIf you own one of the read-only models and can test it with EBCTester, please share your results via the GitHub project so the model can be enabled.

What you need

  • An EBCTester board with installed firmware (as delivered).
  • A supported ZKE tester and a cable from the board's 6-pin connector J4 to the tester's serial (TTL) port.
  • Power: the 19 V supply of the EBC-A20 (through J4) or a USB-C power supply.
  • A 2.4 GHz antenna (the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U module has no on-board antenna).
    •
  • An ESP32-S3 module or board with 16 MB flash (for example ESP32-S3-WROOM-1(U)-N16R8 or an ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 N16R8). The partition table does not fit smaller flash sizes.
  • A bidirectional 3.3 V ↔ 5 V level shifter for the tester UART (the tester uses 5 V TTL; ESP32 pins are not 5 V tolerant).
  • A microSD socket wired for 4-bit SDMMC or SPI, with 10 kΩ pull-ups on the data and command lines.
  • Optionally a WS2812 RGB LED as status indicator.
  • A supported ZKE tester, a suitable cable and a 5 V or USB supply for the ESP32.
  • A PC with a USB cable for the first installation of the firmware.
    •
  • A microSD card formatted as FAT32 (exFAT is not supported). 4–32 GB is ideal.
  • A CR2032 coin cell for the real-time clock (optional).
    •
  • A phone, tablet or computer with a current web browser.

Safety information

WarningBatteries store a lot of energy. Wrong settings, damaged cells or poor connections can cause overheating, fire or explosion. Lithium batteries in particular must never be charged above their maximum voltage or discharged below their minimum voltage.
  • Enter charge voltages, currents and cut-off values that match the battery's data sheet. EBCTester only checks the limits of the tester, not those of your battery.
  • The software does not replace hardware protection (BMS, fuse, temperature monitoring). Long tests are not automatically safe to leave unattended.
  • If the serial connection is lost, EBCTester cannot guarantee that the tester stops. Check the tester locally after any connection error.
  • Use cables and connectors rated for the test current, and keep the test setup on a non-flammable surface.
  • The web interface uses unencrypted HTTP. Use it only in your own trusted network or through the device's protected access point – never expose it to the Internet.
  • Wrong pin assignments can damage your hardware, for example when an output is configured on a pin that is driven by another circuit. Check every GPIO in Maker Settings against your wiring before activating the configuration.
